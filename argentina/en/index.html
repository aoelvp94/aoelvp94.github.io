A guide to enjoying Argentina

Practical tips for friends and visitors, from an Argentine who spent years traveling the world and came back to appreciate what's good about his country. This is not a formal tourist guide — it's what I'd tell someone I know who's coming to visit.

🚕 Getting around

Argentina is big. Buenos Aires has the subte (subway), colectivos (buses), and relatively cheap taxis/Uber. To get between cities, flying is usually the best option — the distances are enormous. Long-distance buses are comfortable and affordable, but be ready for long trips (10–20 hours is not unusual).

Useful apps

  • Cómo Llego — public transit in Buenos Aires
  • BA Móvil / SUBE — to top up the transit card
  • Uber / Cabify / DiDi — work well in big cities
  • Flybondi / JetSmart — low-cost airlines for domestic flights

If you're going to Patagonia, Mendoza, or the north, consider renting a car. The roads are beautiful, but drive carefully — many are mountain routes.

💵 Money and exchange

⚠️ Important warning Argentina's exchange situation changes constantly. What's here is a general guide, but rates, regulations, and apps can vary. Check current sources before you travel.

Historically, Argentina had an official dollar (the bank/ATM rate) and a blue dollar (informal exchange, a better rate). The gap was huge for years. Depending on when you read this, political changes may have unified or rewritten the rules.

Practical advice

  • Bring cash dollars if you can — they've always been useful for getting a better rate
  • Ask a local you trust where to exchange, or use apps like Belo / Let'sBit that operate legally
  • International credit cards apply the official rate, which isn't always the best
  • Many places accept payment by transfer (Mercado Pago, Modo) — handy if you have a local account

Bottom line: don't exchange everything at the airport. Find out first.

🍖 Food, asado, and mate

Food is a central part of Argentine culture. Asado (barbecue) is almost a religion — if you're invited to one, go. It's slow, social, and there's a lot of meat.

What to try

  • Asado — mixed grills, achuras (chinchulines, blood sausage), vacío, chorizo
  • Empanadas — beef, ham and cheese, humita (from the north), or whatever they have
  • Milanesas — huge, with fries. A classic
  • Choripán — chorizo on bread, with chimichurri. Iconic street food
  • Dulce de leche — in everything. Alfajores, ice cream, desserts
  • Wine — Mendoza is the capital. Malbec, of course

Mate

Mate is the shared infusion par excellence. It's not just a drink — it's a social ritual. If they offer you some, accept. Drink what they pass you, say "gracias" when you don't want more, and hand the mate back. Don't stir it, don't say "it's too hot/cold," just drink.

Cafés and bars

Buenos Aires has a strong café culture, with historic bars (Café Tortoni, Los 36 Billares) and modern spots (specialty coffee in Palermo). Coffee is more European than American — cortado, café con leche, lágrima.

📍 Buenos Aires and beyond

Buenos Aires

The capital is huge and diverse. Palermo (cafés, bars, parks), San Telmo (Sunday antique market, tango), Recoleta (famous cemetery, museums), Puerto Madero (renovated, expensive, nice views). La Boca is touristy but fine for a quick visit — watch your things.

Patagonia

The south is spectacular. Bariloche (lakes, mountains, chocolate), El Calafate (Perito Moreno Glacier), Ushuaia (the end of the world), El Chaltén (world-class trekking). Go in summer (December–March) if you want to hike; in winter if you want to ski.

The north

Salta, Jujuy, Quebrada de Humahuaca, the Salinas Grandes. The landscape changes completely — colored mountains, giant cacti, colonial towns. Try Salta-style empanadas and torrontés wine.

Mendoza

Wine and mountains. Winery tours, Aconcagua (the highest mountain in the Americas), Uspallata. If you like wine, it's a must-stop.

Iguazú

Iguazú Falls are in the northeast (Misiones). They're stunning. You can see the Argentine side and cross into Brazil if you have time. Hot and humid — bring insect repellent.

🔒 Safety and street smarts

Argentina isn't a dangerous country in general, but as in any big city, you need to stay alert. Phone and wallet thefts happen, especially in tourist areas and on public transit.

Basic tips

  • Don't take your phone out on the street without paying attention. If you have to use it, do it quickly or somewhere quieter
  • Don't use open backpacks on the subway or buses — pickpockets exist
  • Be careful at night — some areas are fine (Palermo, Recoleta), others less so. Ask before walking alone late at night
  • Don't flash cash on the street — pay discreetly, keep your wallet in a front pocket
  • Taxi from the airport — use official remises or Uber, not random taxis that offer you a ride

That said, most people are kind and helpful. If you're lost, ask — Argentines usually help with directions (even if they sometimes send you the wrong way with total confidence).

🚫 What NOT to do

  • Don't talk about the Malvinas/Falklands if you don't want a political argument
  • Don't compare Argentina with other Latin American countries in a condescending way — Argentines like to set themselves apart (for better or worse)
  • Don't expect British punctuality — "I'll be there in 10 minutes" is usually 30. It's cultural
  • Don't ask for "a coffee" without specifying — ask what they have. "Café americano" exists but isn't the most common
  • Don't automatically leave a 10% tip — check whether the "cubierto" or table service is already included
  • Don't take offense if they call you "che" or "boludo" — they're colloquial forms of address, not insults (depends on the tone, of course)

🌦️ Best time to visit

It depends on where you're going. Argentina has opposite climates in different regions:

  • Buenos Aires — spring (Sep–Nov) and fall (Mar–May) are ideal. Summer (Dec–Feb) is hot and humid; winter (Jun–Aug) is cool but not extreme
  • Patagonia — summer (Dec–Mar) for trekking; winter (Jun–Sep) for snow and skiing
  • The north (Salta, Jujuy) — avoid summer (Dec–Feb) if you don't like extreme heat. Fall/spring are better
  • Mendoza — spring and fall are ideal for wineries; winter for skiing at Las Leñas
  • Iguazú — year-round, but it's tropical — always hot and humid. Spring has a higher water volume

Remember that the seasons are reversed (Southern Hemisphere) — December is summer, July is winter.

🤝 One last tip

Argentines can be intense, direct, and full of opinions (about soccer, politics, asado, anything). But they're also warm, generous with food and time, and they'll welcome you well if you come with good vibes.

Argentina has its quilombos — inflation, chaotic politics, bureaucracy — but it also has incredible landscapes, excellent food, and people who will go out of their way to make you feel at home.

Enjoy it. And bring dollars. 😉
